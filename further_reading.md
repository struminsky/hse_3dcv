## Lecture 1 — Image Formation

A few links following up on the first lecture:

1. **Perspective tricks in cinema** — A great breakdown of how filmmakers play with perspective:

## Lecture 2 — Multi-View Geometry

A few links following up on this lecture:

1. **COLMAP** — The go-to open-source tool for structure from motion and multi-view stereo reconstruction:
   [https://colmap.github.io/](https://colmap.github.io/)

2. **RealityCapture** — A free tool from Epic Games with a more polished UI, well-suited for practical use:
   [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RealityCapture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RealityCapture)

3. **GLOMAP** — A recent take on global structure from motion, offering a more parallelizable alternative to conventional sequential SfM. One of the key ideas is replacing the standard 2D reprojection loss with a camera ray rotation error estimated directly in 3D:
   [https://github.com/colmap/glomap](https://github.com/colmap/glomap)

## Lecture 6 — Polygon Meshes and Rendering Algorithms

A few links following up on today's lecture:

1. **Lights and Shadows** — Interactive web demos and a detailed post on how light, shadows, and reflections work:
   [https://ciechanow.ski/lights-and-shadows/](https://ciechanow.ski/lights-and-shadows/)

2. **Path tracing in JAX** — An implementation of path tracing using JAX:
   [https://blog.evjang.com/2019/11/jaxpt.html](https://blog.evjang.com/2019/11/jaxpt.html)

3. **Ray tracing and acceleration algorithms** — A video covering ray tracing and the algorithms that make it fast:
   [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gsZiJeaMO48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gsZiJeaMO48)

4. **Physically Based Rendering: From Theory to Implementation** — The definitive book on physically based rendering, written in the literate programming style. It won an Academy Award, and Donald Knuth once quipped it deserved a Pulitzer Prize nomination too:
   [https://www.pbrt.org/](https://www.pbrt.org/)

5. **Rendering geometry that isn't there** — We talked about normal maps in the lecture; the same principle is behind rendering entire apartment floors in the infinite skyscrapers that Spider-Man climbs:
   [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eX7x1mJrQJs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eX7x1mJrQJs)

6. **Deferred Neural Shading** — We ran a little short on time when discussing this at the end of the lecture, but the authors' demo is well worth a look on its own:
   [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ofVgAEb1FiE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ofVgAEb1FiE)
